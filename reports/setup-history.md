Grafana's data-source proxy returned both metrics and logs, and the four-panel dashboard was provisioned. ## k0s experiment (2026-11-24 UTC)

k0s v1.37.2+k0s.1 was installed alongside the preserved K3s installation. The k0s systemd unit uses `--single`, a separate `/var/lib/k0s/kubelet` root, `--kubelet-extra-args=--fail-swap-on=false`, and disables CoreDNS and metrics-server. Its service is stopped and disabled. Never run both controllers simultaneously: both use API port 6443 and can leave containers and networking state behind after stopping. Kubernetes runtime and firewall state should be inspected before switching; reboot recovery and switching back have not been tested. The k0s-specific manifests in `kubernetes/k0s/` are preparation only and were not verified end to end. The observability VM still has the original K3s credentials.

The first k0s run was contaminated by K3s sample and Fluent Bit processes that survived stopping K3s, so it is not a clean comparison. After those processes were stopped, a retry reached a Ready node with kube-proxy and kube-router running, 420 MiB available and 100 MiB swap used. While applying the sample manifest, only the namespace was confirmed created before the API stalled; swap rose to 349 MiB, memory pressure rose, and one `vmstat` sample showed 61% CPU steal. The application and Fluent Bit were not validated on k0s, and observability was not switched to it. k0s was stopped, its remaining kube-router and kube-proxy processes were terminated, and automatic startup remained disabled. Afterward the VM showed 626 MiB available and 103 MiB swap used. A few containerd shim processes remained without the identified workload processes. These short tests do not establish comparative steady-state resource use.

## Compressed swap trial (2026-11-24 UTC)

Zswap was temporarily enabled at runtime with its existing 20% maximum pool and K3s was started with a five-minute stop timer. The pre-test baseline was 633 MiB available and 103 MiB of disk swap in use; both controllers were stopped. Once K3s and the sample and Fluent Bit processes were running, one snapshot showed 274 MiB available, 165 MiB swap in use, and approximately 10 MiB held by zswap representing 23 MiB of uncompressed pages. Ten-second memory PSI `some` was 48%; two `vmstat` samples showed 52% and 60% CPU steal. These are short startup samples, not a controlled steady-state comparison; they do not establish that zswap caused or failed to improve the problem. The trial was stopped early because of pressure. At that point K3s and k0s were stopped and disabled, and the identified sample and Fluent Bit processes were stopped. Zswap was switched back off. Disabling it prevents new stores; previously compressed pages may remain until faulted in or invalidated. No persistent zswap configuration was added.

Potential further experiments: run administrative clients off-node; compare one change at a time against an equivalent baseline over several minutes, tracking API latency, PSI, CPU steal, swap-in/out and OOM events; reduce scrape frequency or temporarily stop Fluent Bit if appropriate. Do not disable Oracle's agent, iSCSI, SSH or other existing services just to recover memory without first checking their role. Kubernetes defaults to `NoSwap` for pods even when `failSwapOn=false`, while host services may still swap.

## K3s tuning trial (2026-11-24 UTC)

Existing add-on disables and SQLite were retained. `/etc/rancher/k3s/config.yaml` adds kube-apiserver concurrency flags of 50 and 25; `/etc/systemd/system/k3s.service.d/90-sre-lab-tuning.conf` sets `GOGC=50` and `GOMEMLIMIT=450MiB`. These two files persist, while `sudo sysctl -w vm.swappiness=10` is temporary and returns to the system default after reboot. Go's memory limit is a soft target, applies to Go runtime-managed memory rather than process RSS or whole-node usage, and environment variables may also be inherited by child processes. More frequent garbage collection can consume CPU.

The node, sample and Fluent Bit became Ready; `kubectl get all -A` succeeded, the sample returned HTTP 200, and Prometheus reported its K3s target up with a 1.3-second scrape. In a roughly three-minute observation, K3s RSS ranged approximately 359–396 MiB, available memory was approximately 310 MiB at the final snapshot, and swap in use rose from 103 to 204 MiB. CPU steal reached 67–77% in some startup samples and later fell near zero in some samples; swap-in and swap-out were intermittently nonzero. The initial zswap trial and this tuning trial were too short and different for a controlled attribution; no long-term stability or fixed CPU/RSS ceiling is established. CoreDNS remains disabled, so pod DNS is unavailable.

To undo this experiment on the Kubernetes VM, stop K3s, remove only `/etc/rancher/k3s/config.yaml` and `/etc/systemd/system/k3s.service.d/90-sre-lab-tuning.conf`, run `sudo systemctl daemon-reload`, and restore the previous runtime value with `sudo sysctl -w vm.swappiness=60`. Review these files before removal if they have since been edited. Stop any surviving lab workload containers as needed.

## Interrupted soak and memory guard (2026-11-24 UTC)

A 30-minute soak began at 07:01:44 UTC and was stopped at the user's request at approximately 07:17 UTC, so it did not complete. `vmstat` captured 30 complete 30-second intervals after its cumulative first line. Swap-in was nonzero in all 30 intervals (median 8 KiB/s, max 124 KiB/s); swap-out was zero in 20 intervals (max 213 KiB/s). CPU steal reached 28% in a 30-second average. K3s RSS across 31 point samples was approximately 338–366 MiB. The Prometheus `k3s-node` series contained seven successful `up` samples in the last 15 minutes at the stop, with a maximum reported scrape duration of 0.071 seconds; this does not prove complete coverage outside those observed samples. The `apiserver_request_duration_seconds` metric was not scraped. Raw logs are in `reports/soak-2026-11-24/`.

`MemoryHigh=500M` was applied live with `systemctl set-property --runtime` and added to the existing K3s service drop-in for future starts. It applies to the service cgroup and descendants, including containerd and shims, not just the K3s server process; `memory.high` can throttle and reclaim rather than enforce a hard cap. At stop, `MemoryCurrent` was about 496 MiB and the cgroup had counted 443 `high` events and no OOM kills during observation. The sampler was stopped; K3s and the observability stack were left running, and K3s remains disabled at boot. `vm.swappiness=10` remains a runtime setting only and was not persisted because the requested full soak did not pass or finish.
